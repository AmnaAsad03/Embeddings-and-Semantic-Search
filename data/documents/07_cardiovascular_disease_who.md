# Cardiovascular Diseases

## Overview

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are the leading cause of death globally, taking an estimated 17.9 million lives each year. CVDs are a group of disorders of the heart and blood vessels and include coronary heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, rheumatic heart disease, and other conditions.

More than four out of five CVD deaths are due to heart attacks and strokes, and one third of these deaths occur prematurely in people under 70 years of age.

The most important behavioural risk factors for heart disease and stroke are unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, tobacco use, and harmful use of alcohol. Air pollution is also an important environmental risk factor.

The effects of behavioural risk factors may appear as raised blood pressure, raised blood glucose, raised blood lipids, and overweight and obesity. These intermediate risk factors can be measured in primary care facilities and indicate an increased risk of heart attack, stroke, heart failure, and other complications.

Identifying people at highest risk of CVDs and ensuring they receive appropriate treatment can help prevent premature deaths. Access to appropriate medicines and basic health technologies in primary health care facilities is essential for people who need treatment and counselling.

## Symptoms

### Heart Attack

Often, there are no symptoms of the underlying disease of the blood vessels. A heart attack may be the first sign of underlying cardiovascular disease.

Symptoms of a heart attack include:

- Pain or discomfort in the centre of the chest.
- Pain or discomfort in the arms, left shoulder, elbows, jaw, or back.
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath.
- Nausea or vomiting.
- Light-headedness or faintness.
- Cold sweat.
- Turning pale.

Women are more likely than men to experience shortness of breath, nausea, vomiting, and back or jaw pain.

### Stroke

The most common symptom of a stroke is sudden weakness of the face, arm, or leg, most often on one side of the body.

Other symptoms include sudden onset of:

- Numbness of the face, arm, or leg, especially on one side of the body.
- Confusion or difficulty speaking or understanding speech.
- Difficulty seeing with one or both eyes.
- Difficulty walking, dizziness, or loss of balance or coordination.
- Severe headache with no known cause.
- Fainting or unconsciousness.

People experiencing symptoms of a heart attack or stroke should seek medical care immediately.

### Rheumatic Heart Disease

Symptoms of rheumatic heart disease include:

- Shortness of breath.
- Fatigue.
- Irregular heartbeats.
- Chest pain.
- Fainting.

Symptoms of rheumatic fever, which can cause rheumatic heart disease if not treated, include:

- Fever.
- Pain and swelling of the joints.
- Nausea.
- Stomach cramps.
- Vomiting.

## Causes and Risk Factors

Important behavioural risk factors for cardiovascular disease include:

- Tobacco use.
- Unhealthy diet.
- Harmful use of alcohol.
- Inadequate physical activity.

An important environmental risk factor is air pollution.

Physiological risk factors include:

- High blood pressure (hypertension).
- High blood cholesterol.
- High blood sugar or glucose.
- Overweight and obesity.

These risk factors are linked to underlying social determinants and drivers such as ageing, income, and urbanization.

## Treatment and Management

WHO supports governments in preventing, managing, and monitoring cardiovascular diseases through strategies that include:

- Reducing cardiovascular disease risk factors.
- Developing standards of care.
- Enhancing health system capacity to care for patients with cardiovascular disease.
- Monitoring disease patterns and trends to inform national and global actions.

Identifying people at high risk of cardiovascular disease and ensuring that they receive appropriate treatment can help prevent premature deaths.

Access to noncommunicable disease medicines and basic health technologies in primary health care facilities is essential to ensure that people who need treatment and counselling can receive them.

## Prevention and Risk Reduction

The following measures have been shown to reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease:

- Stopping tobacco use.
- Reducing salt in the diet.
- Eating more fruits and vegetables.
- Engaging in regular physical activity.
- Avoiding harmful use of alcohol.

Health policies that make healthy choices affordable and available, improve air quality, and reduce pollution are also important for supporting healthy behaviours.

## When to Seek Medical Care

People experiencing symptoms of a heart attack or stroke should seek medical care immediately.

Particularly important warning symptoms include:

- Chest pain or discomfort.
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath.
- Sudden weakness or numbness, especially on one side of the body.
- Sudden confusion or difficulty speaking.
- Sudden difficulty seeing.
- Sudden difficulty walking, dizziness, or loss of balance.
- Severe headache with no known cause.
- Fainting or unconsciousness.

## Additional Information

Cardiovascular diseases include a group of disorders affecting the heart and blood vessels. Major forms include coronary heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, and rheumatic heart disease.

Reducing behavioural and environmental risk factors, identifying people at high risk, and ensuring access to appropriate treatment and counselling are important components of cardiovascular disease prevention and management.
