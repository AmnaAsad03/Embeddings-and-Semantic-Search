# Food Poisoning

## Overview

Food poisoning occurs when people consume foods containing germs that can make them sick.

Some foods are more likely than others to contain harmful germs. These risky foods can spread germs to people, surfaces in the home, and other foods.

To reduce the risk of food poisoning, follow four main food-safety steps:

1. Clean.
2. Separate.
3. Cook.
4. Chill.

If a food is recalled, it should be thrown away.

## Symptoms

Food poisoning symptoms can include:

- Diarrhea.
- Stomach pain or cramps.
- Nausea.
- Vomiting.
- Fever.

## Causes

Food poisoning can result from consuming food contaminated with harmful germs.

Foods that are more likely to contain harmful germs include:

- Raw or undercooked poultry, meat, seafood, or eggs.
- Raw or undercooked sprouts.
- Unwashed fruits and vegetables.
- Cut melon.
- Unpasteurized milk, juice, cider, or soft cheese.
- Raw dough or raw batter made with uncooked flour.

## Risk Factors

The risk of food poisoning can increase when food is:

- Raw or undercooked.
- Not washed properly.
- Cross-contaminated with raw meat, poultry, seafood, or eggs.
- Not cooked to a safe internal temperature.
- Left at room temperature for too long.
- Thawed improperly.

## Prevention

The CDC recommends four main steps for preventing food poisoning: **Clean, Separate, Cook, and Chill.**

### 1. Clean

Germs that cause food poisoning can survive in many places and spread around the kitchen.

- Wash hands before, during, and after preparing food and before eating.
- Wash hands for at least 20 seconds with soap and warm or cold water.
- Wash utensils, cutting boards, and countertops with hot, soapy water after preparing each food item.
- Rinse fresh fruits and vegetables under running water.
- Always wash hands after handling uncooked meat, chicken and other poultry, seafood, flour, or eggs.

Raw chicken does not need to be washed before cooking. Washing raw chicken can spread germs to other foods, the sink, and countertops.

### 2. Separate

Raw meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs can spread germs to ready-to-eat foods unless they are kept separate.

#### When Grocery Shopping

Keep raw meat, poultry, seafood, and their juices away from other foods.

#### When Storing Food

Keep raw or marinating meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs separate from other foods.

Store raw meat, poultry, and seafood in sealed containers or wrap them securely so their juices do not leak onto other foods.

#### When Preparing Food

Use one cutting board or plate for raw meat, poultry, and seafood and a separate cutting board or plate for produce, bread, and other foods that will not be cooked.

### 3. Cook

Food is safely cooked when its internal temperature becomes high enough to kill harmful germs.

A food thermometer is the reliable way to determine whether food has reached a safe internal temperature. Color and texture alone cannot reliably indicate whether food is safely cooked.

Recommended safe internal temperatures include:

| Food | Safe Internal Temperature |
|---|---:|
| Whole cuts of beef, veal, lamb, and pork, including fresh ham | 145°F, followed by a 3-minute rest |
| Fish with fins | 145°F or until the flesh is opaque and separates easily with a fork |
| Ground meats, such as beef and pork | 160°F |
| All poultry, including ground chicken and turkey | 165°F |
| Leftovers and casseroles | 165°F |

#### Microwave Food Thoroughly

Follow recommended cooking and standing times when microwaving food. Allowing food to stand for a few minutes after microwaving helps cold spots absorb heat from hotter areas.

When reheating food in a microwave, use a food thermometer to make sure the food reaches 165°F.

### 4. Chill

Bacteria can multiply rapidly when food is left at room temperature or in the temperature range between 40°F and 140°F.

- Never leave perishable food out for more than 2 hours.
- If food is exposed to temperatures above 90°F, do not leave it out for more than 1 hour.
- Keep the refrigerator at 40°F or below.
- Keep the freezer at 0°F or below.
- Refrigerate perishable foods within 2 hours.
- Refrigerate perishable foods within 1 hour if exposed to temperatures above 90°F.

Perishable foods include meat, seafood, dairy products, cut fruit, some vegetables, and cooked leftovers.

Warm or hot food can be divided into several clean, shallow containers and refrigerated so it can cool more quickly.

#### Thaw Food Properly

Safely thaw frozen food:

- In the refrigerator.
- In cold water.
- In the microwave.

Do not thaw food on the counter because bacteria can multiply quickly in portions of food that reach room temperature.

## Recalled Food

If food has been recalled:

- Throw the recalled food away.
- Clean the refrigerator if the recalled food was stored there.



## Additional Information

Food poisoning can be prevented by reducing exposure to harmful germs during food preparation, storage, and handling.

The four main food-safety principles are:

- **Clean:** Keep hands and surfaces clean.
- **Separate:** Keep raw and ready-to-eat foods apart.
- **Cook:** Cook foods to safe internal temperatures.
- **Chill:** Refrigerate perishable foods promptly.

Following these steps can help reduce the risk of food poisoning.
