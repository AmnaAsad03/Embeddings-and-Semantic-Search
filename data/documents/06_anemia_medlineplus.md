# Anemia

**Also called: Iron poor blood**

## Overview

Anemia is a condition in which the blood does not carry enough oxygen to the rest of the body.

The most common cause of anemia is not having enough iron. The body needs iron to make hemoglobin. Hemoglobin is an iron-rich protein that gives blood its red color and carries oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body.

Anemia has three main causes:

- Blood loss
- Lack of red blood cell production
- High rates of red blood cell destruction

Conditions that may lead to anemia include:

- Heavy menstrual periods
- Pregnancy
- Ulcers
- Colon polyps or colon cancer
- Inherited disorders
- A diet that does not contain enough iron, folic acid, or vitamin B12
- Blood disorders such as sickle cell anemia and thalassemia
- Cancer
- Aplastic anemia, which can be inherited or acquired
- G6PD deficiency, a metabolic disorder

Anemia can cause symptoms such as tiredness, feeling cold, dizziness, irritability, shortness of breath, and headache.

Treatment depends on the type and cause of anemia.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of anemia may include:

- Tiredness or fatigue
- Feeling cold
- Dizziness
- Irritability
- Shortness of breath
- Headache

The symptoms may vary depending on the type and cause of anemia.

## Causes

Anemia can develop when:

1. The body loses blood.
2. The body does not produce enough red blood cells.
3. Red blood cells are destroyed at a high rate.

Possible conditions and factors associated with anemia include:

- Heavy menstrual periods
- Pregnancy
- Ulcers
- Colon polyps
- Colon cancer
- Inherited disorders
- Insufficient dietary iron
- Insufficient folic acid
- Insufficient vitamin B12
- Sickle cell anemia
- Thalassemia
- Cancer
- Aplastic anemia
- G6PD deficiency

Iron deficiency is the most common cause of anemia.

## Risk Factors

Factors and conditions that may increase the risk of anemia include:

- Heavy menstrual bleeding
- Pregnancy
- A diet lacking sufficient iron, folic acid, or vitamin B12
- Inherited blood disorders
- Chronic or serious medical conditions
- Conditions that cause blood loss
- Conditions that increase red blood cell destruction
- Certain cancers

The specific risk factors depend on the type of anemia.

## Diagnosis

A health care provider diagnoses anemia using a medical history, physical examination, and blood tests.

Tests that may be used to evaluate anemia include:

- Complete blood count (CBC)
- Blood smear
- Iron tests
- Ferritin blood test
- Vitamin B12 testing
- Reticulocyte count
- Hemoglobin electrophoresis
- Haptoglobin testing
- Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) testing
- Bone marrow tests

The tests used depend on the suspected type and cause of anemia.

## Treatment

Treatment depends on the type and underlying cause of anemia.

Treatment may involve addressing the cause of anemia and replacing nutrients that are lacking, such as:

- Iron
- Folic acid
- Vitamin B12

Some types of anemia require treatment for an underlying medical condition or blood disorder.

Treatment should be determined based on the specific type and cause of anemia.

## Prevention

Some types of anemia may be prevented by addressing nutritional deficiencies and other known causes.

Maintaining adequate intake of nutrients such as iron, folic acid, and vitamin B12 may help prevent anemia caused by nutritional deficiencies.

Prevention depends on the specific type and cause of anemia.


## When to Seek Medical Care

People experiencing symptoms such as persistent fatigue, dizziness, shortness of breath, headaches, or other possible signs of anemia should discuss them with a health care provider.

Medical evaluation is important because anemia can have many different causes, including blood loss, nutritional deficiencies, inherited disorders, and other medical conditions.

## Additional Information

Anemia is a broad condition with many different types and causes. Identifying the underlying cause is important because treatment varies according to the type of anemia.
