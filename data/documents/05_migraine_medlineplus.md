# Migraine

## Overview

Migraines are a recurring type of headache. They cause moderate to severe pain that is throbbing or pulsing. The pain is often on one side of the head. Migraines may also cause nausea, weakness, and sensitivity to light and sound.

There is no cure for migraines, but treatment can help relieve symptoms and prevent additional attacks.

## Symptoms

Migraines can have four different phases, although a person may not experience every phase during each migraine.

### Prodrome

The prodrome phase can begin up to 24 hours before the migraine. Early signs and symptoms may include:

- Food cravings
- Unexplained mood changes
- Uncontrollable yawning
- Fluid retention
- Increased urination

### Aura

Some people experience an aura just before or during a migraine. Symptoms may include:

- Seeing flashing or bright lights
- Seeing zig-zag lines
- Muscle weakness
- Sensations of being touched or grabbed

### Headache

A migraine usually starts gradually and becomes more severe. The pain is typically throbbing or pulsing and is often on one side of the head.

Other symptoms may include:

- Increased sensitivity to light, noise, and odors
- Nausea and vomiting
- Pain that becomes worse with movement, coughing, or sneezing

Some people can experience a migraine without a headache.

### Postdrome

After the headache, a person may feel:

- Exhausted
- Weak
- Confused

These symptoms can last up to a day.

Migraines are more common in the morning, and some people wake up with them. Some people experience migraines at predictable times, such as before menstruation or on weekends following a stressful week.

## Causes

Researchers believe that migraines have a genetic cause. Various factors can trigger migraine attacks, and triggers can differ from person to person.

Possible triggers include:

- Stress
- Anxiety
- Hormonal changes in women
- Bright or flashing lights
- Loud noises
- Strong smells
- Certain medicines
- Too much or too little sleep
- Sudden changes in weather or environment
- Overexertion or excessive physical activity
- Tobacco
- Caffeine or caffeine withdrawal
- Skipped meals
- Medication overuse, such as taking migraine medicine too often

Some people report that certain foods or ingredients can trigger headaches, especially when combined with other triggers. These may include:

- Alcohol
- Chocolate
- Aged cheeses
- Monosodium glutamate (MSG)
- Some fruits and nuts
- Fermented or pickled foods
- Yeast
- Cured or processed meats

## Risk Factors

Migraines can affect anyone, but certain factors can increase the likelihood of having them.

Risk factors include:

- Being a woman. Women are about three times more likely than men to experience migraines.
- Having a family history of migraines. Most people with migraines have family members who also experience them.
- Having other medical conditions, such as depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, sleep disorders, or epilepsy.

## Diagnosis

To diagnose migraines, a health care provider may:

- Take a medical history
- Ask about symptoms
- Perform a physical examination
- Perform a neurological examination

An important part of diagnosis is ruling out other medical conditions that could be causing the symptoms.

Additional tests may include:

- Blood tests
- Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)
- Computed tomography (CT) scan
- Other tests when needed

## Treatment

Treatment focuses on relieving symptoms and preventing additional attacks.

### Medicines

Medicines used to relieve migraine symptoms may include:

- Triptan drugs
- Ergotamine drugs
- Pain relievers

Migraine medicines are generally more effective when taken early in an attack.

People with frequent or severe migraines may need medicines to prevent additional attacks. The appropriate preventive medicine should be discussed with a health care provider.

### Self-Care During a Migraine

Other measures that may help relieve symptoms include:

- Resting with the eyes closed in a quiet, darkened room
- Placing a cool cloth or ice pack on the forehead
- Drinking fluids

### Lifestyle and Prevention Strategies

Lifestyle changes may help reduce the number or severity of migraine attacks.

Stress-management strategies such as the following may be helpful:

- Exercise
- Relaxation techniques
- Biofeedback

Biofeedback uses electronic devices to teach a person to control certain body functions, such as heartbeat, blood pressure, and muscle tension.

Keeping a log of migraine triggers can help identify factors that should be avoided. A migraine log may also help identify useful habits, such as:

- Maintaining a consistent sleep schedule
- Eating regular meals
- Avoiding identified food or medication triggers

Hormone therapy may help some women whose migraines appear to be associated with the menstrual cycle.

For people with obesity, weight loss may also be helpful.

### Supplements and Natural Treatments

Certain natural treatments may help prevent migraines, including:

- Riboflavin (vitamin B2)
- Coenzyme Q10
- Magnesium when magnesium levels are low

Some people use the herb butterbur to prevent migraines, but it may not be safe for long-term use.

People should consult a health care provider before taking supplements or herbal treatments.

## Prevention

Migraine prevention may involve identifying and avoiding individual triggers.

Helpful strategies may include:

- Managing stress
- Maintaining a consistent sleep schedule
- Eating regular meals
- Keeping a record of migraine triggers
- Avoiding identified food, medicine, or environmental triggers
- Maintaining physical activity when appropriate
- Discussing preventive medicines with a health care provider when migraines are frequent or severe


## When to Seek Medical Care

People with frequent or severe migraines should talk with a health care provider about preventive treatment and which medicines may be appropriate.

A health care provider should also be consulted before using supplements or herbal treatments for migraine prevention.
