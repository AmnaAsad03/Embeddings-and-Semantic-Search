# Pneumonia

## Overview

Pneumonia is a form of acute respiratory infection most commonly caused by viruses or bacteria. It can cause mild to life-threatening illness in people of all ages and is the single largest infectious cause of death in children worldwide.

Pneumonia killed more than 808,000 children under the age of 5 in 2017, accounting for 15% of all deaths of children under 5 years.

People at increased risk for pneumonia include:

- Children, particularly those under 5 years of age.
- Adults over the age of 65.
- People with preexisting health problems.

The lungs contain small sacs called alveoli, which fill with air when a healthy person breathes. When a person has pneumonia, the alveoli become filled with pus and fluid. This makes breathing painful and limits oxygen intake.

Pneumonia infections are generally spread through direct contact with infected people.

Vaccines can help prevent pneumonia.

## Symptoms

Depending on the severity of pneumonia, signs and symptoms may include:

- Cough.
- Shortness of breath.
- Fever.
- Sweating and shaking chills.
- Fatigue.
- Chest pain.
- Nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea.
- Confusion, especially in older adults.

## Causes

Pneumonia is an acute respiratory infection most commonly caused by:

- Viruses.
- Bacteria.

The infection can cause the alveoli in the lungs to fill with pus and fluid, limiting oxygen intake and making breathing painful.

Pneumonia infections are generally spread through direct contact with infected people.

## Risk Factors

People at increased risk for pneumonia include:

- Children under 5 years of age.
- Adults over 65 years of age.
- People with preexisting health problems.

Adequate nutrition is important for maintaining natural defenses against pneumonia.



## Treatment

Bacterial pneumonia may be treated with antibiotics. Amoxicillin dispersible tablets are identified by WHO as the antibiotic of choice.

Most cases of pneumonia require oral antibiotics, which are often prescribed at a health centre. These cases can also be diagnosed and treated at the community level by trained community health workers using inexpensive oral antibiotics.

Hospitalization is recommended for severe cases of pneumonia.

Rest and adequate hydration can also help people recover.

## Prevention

People of all ages can reduce their risk of pneumonia through the following measures:

### Vaccination

Immunization against the following diseases is an effective way to prevent pneumonia:

- Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib).
- Pneumococcus.
- Measles.
- Influenza.
- Whooping cough (pertussis).

### Hand Hygiene

Maintain good hygiene and wash hands frequently, especially when caring for people who are sick or after blowing your nose, to help prevent the spread of germs.

### Environmental Factors

Reducing indoor air pollution can help reduce the risk of pneumonia. This includes providing access to affordable clean indoor stoves and avoiding smoking.

### Healthy Lifestyle

Maintaining a healthy lifestyle can support overall health. This includes:

- Eating a healthy diet.
- Getting adequate rest.
- Engaging in regular exercise.

### Nutrition and Breastfeeding

Adequate nutrition is important for improving natural defenses. For infants, exclusive breastfeeding during the first 6 months of life is recommended in the provided WHO content.




## Additional Information

Pneumonia is an acute respiratory infection that primarily affects the lungs. It can affect people of all ages and is particularly important as a cause of illness and death among young children.

Vaccination, good hand hygiene, reducing indoor air pollution, avoiding smoking, maintaining a healthy lifestyle, and adequate nutrition are important measures for reducing the risk of pneumonia.
