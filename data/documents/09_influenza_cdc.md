# Influenza (Flu)

## Overview

Influenza (also known as "flu") is a contagious respiratory illness caused by influenza viruses.

Flu can cause mild to severe illness and, at times, can lead to death. Symptoms usually come on suddenly.

Most people who get flu recover in a few days to less than two weeks. However, some people develop complications that can be serious or life-threatening.

## Symptoms

People with flu may experience some or all of the following symptoms:

- Fever or feeling feverish/chills.
- Cough.
- Sore throat.
- Runny or stuffy nose.
- Muscle or body aches.
- Headache.
- Fatigue (tiredness).
- Vomiting and diarrhea, which are more common in children than adults.

Not everyone with flu will have a fever.

## Causes

Influenza is caused by influenza viruses.

It is a contagious respiratory illness and can cause illness ranging from mild to severe.

## Risk Factors

Anyone can get sick with flu, including healthy people. Serious flu-related complications can occur at any age.

People at higher risk of developing serious flu-related complications include:

- Adults 65 years and older.
- People of any age with certain chronic medical conditions, such as asthma, diabetes, or heart disease.
- Pregnant women.
- Children younger than 5 years, especially children younger than 2 years.


## Complications

Most people recover from flu without serious complications, but some develop complications that can be serious or life-threatening.

Possible flu complications include:

- Pneumonia.
- Sinus infections.
- Ear infections.
- Inflammation of the heart (myocarditis).
- Inflammation of the brain (encephalitis).
- Inflammation of muscle tissues (myositis).
- Rhabdomyolysis.
- Multi-organ failure, such as respiratory and kidney failure.
- Sepsis.

Flu infection can also worsen existing chronic medical conditions. For example:

- People with asthma may experience asthma attacks.
- People with chronic heart disease may experience worsening of their condition.

Pneumonia can result from influenza virus infection alone or from co-infection with influenza virus and bacteria.

## Cold Versus Flu

| Sign or Symptom | Cold | Influenza (Flu) |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom onset | Gradual | Abrupt |
| Fever | Rare | Common; lasts 3–4 days |
| Aches | Slight | Common; often severe |
| Chills | Uncommon | Fairly common |
| Fatigue and weakness | Sometimes | Usual |
| Sneezing | Common | Sometimes |
| Chest discomfort and cough | Mild to moderate; hacking cough | Common; can be severe |
| Stuffy nose | Common | Sometimes |
| Sore throat | Common | Sometimes |
| Headache | Rare | Common |

## When to Seek Medical Care

People experiencing emergency warning signs should obtain medical care right away.

### Emergency Warning Signs in Children

- Fast breathing or trouble breathing.
- Bluish lips or face.
- Ribs pulling in with each breath.
- Chest pain.
- Severe muscle pain, such as refusing to walk.
- Signs of dehydration, including no urine for 8 hours, dry mouth, or no tears when crying.
- Not alert or interacting when awake.
- Seizures.
- Fever above 104°F that is not controlled by fever-reducing medicine.
- Any fever in children younger than 12 weeks.
- Fever or cough that improves but then returns or worsens.
- Worsening of chronic medical conditions.

### Emergency Warning Signs in Adults

- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath.
- Persistent pain or pressure in the chest or abdomen.
- Persistent dizziness, confusion, or inability to arouse.
- Seizures.
- Not urinating.
- Severe muscle pain.
- Severe weakness or unsteadiness.
- Fever or cough that improves but then returns or worsens.
- Worsening of chronic medical conditions.

## Additional Information

Flu symptoms generally appear suddenly and may range from mild to severe. Although most people recover within a few days to less than two weeks, certain groups are at greater risk of serious complications.

Pneumonia is one of the serious complications of flu, while other complications can involve the heart, brain, muscles, kidneys, respiratory system, or multiple organs.
